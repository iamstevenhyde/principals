Principals: what to change

Think piece, 8 Nov 2026. No code changed. Read against commit 1b6ee7a, content/RULES.md, the five board files, the 44 gallery screenshots, and the Jensen Pharma student handout and teaching note (BUS 450, Hill Schilling Jones Chap 11).

Recommendation. Make each round one real board crisis that students recognise (Vioxx-style), let each board make one big call plus the fact vote, and turn the projector into a newspaper front page that writes itself from the boards' choices. Then save ten minutes for a Jensen-style debrief that is built into the game: the reveal walks four questions, one screen each. Drop the Reporter seat and let the game play the press.

1. What Jensen does well

DecisionJensen is Vioxx in disguise. Borrow that.

The teaching note says it outright: the role play "reflects the ambiguity Merck's and Pfizer's boards may have experienced" over Vioxx, Bextra and Celebrex. Students get a fictional firm (Jensen, Dekanor) and a real event underneath it. The debrief then reveals the real case, and that moment gives students the "this actually happened" feeling.

3. Where students get confused

FixEight confusion points, in play order
  1. "100.0" in the header. Nothing says it is the stock price.
  2. The card says "You lose" and "Your secret" but not how points work. Student sees 7.5 pts at the end with no rule to check it against.
  3. Facts screen: why would I switch a fact off? The cost of admitting (stock drop) is not shown, only a "Risk" dial with no unit.
  4. Severity bars double as "how bad" and "how findable". Students read only one meaning.
  5. Statement step is "never scored". Students who learn that will skip it, which drains the newspaper idea unless it gets used.
  6. Motion stakeholder icons ("Contract", "Home", "public") change meaning per board. Users is the "home" community at Lumen.
  7. Chair power (call, close early, break ties) is shown as a badge only.
  8. Reveal: "Gained: nobody Paid: The public" and "exposure safe secret missed" read as code. "Failed to decide" appears without explanation in nearly every round, which suggests the 240 s timer is too short.

Headline = template from passed motion + disclosure level. The board's statement becomes the pull quote, which finally gives the statement a job. The game plays the Reporter, so class size stops mattering.

Pro: one glance, big type, very little reading. The debrief opener writes itself ("whose headline would you want?"). It mirrors Jensen's real-press reveal.

Con: headline templates take writing time (5 boards x 2 rounds x ~4 outcomes). Hidden facts must stay hidden until caught, or it spoils the next round.

DebriefReveal as four screens, mapped to Jensen
  1. Reactions: front page, both rounds.
  2. Governance: side by side (concept C): who told, who got caught.
  3. Fixes: the real event behind each board, one photo and one line.
  4. Ethics: secrets revealed board by board, one board per click.
