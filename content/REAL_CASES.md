## Valley Health

**Round 1: Columbia/HCA Medicare fraud case. PARTLY verified.**
- Used on screen: HCA subsidiaries pleaded guilty to billing fraud in 2000, including upcoding, and recoveries reached about $1.7B by 2003.
- Dec 2000: guilty plea, about $840M ($95M criminal fines, $745M civil), covering upcoding of pneumonia diagnoses (search). https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2000/December/696civcrm.htm
- 2003: $631M civil settlement plus $250M Medicare cost-report payment, about $1.7B total (search). https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2003/June/03_civ_386.htm
- Mar 1997 search warrants; Rick Scott left in July 1997. https://politifact.com/factchecks/2010/may/21/bill-mccollum/rick-scott-former-healthcare-ceo-barely-escaped-pr/
- Dropped: "forced out". PolitiFact says what happened at the July 1997 board meeting is unknown. Board role UNVERIFIED.

**Round 2: Steward Health Care collapse. PARTLY verified; board angle UNVERIFIED.**
- Used on screen: Steward went bankrupt owing about $9B, including $6.6B of rent, and closed hospitals such as Nashoba Valley.
- About $9B debt including $6.6B long-term rent owed to Medical Properties Trust (search). https://www.wusf.org/health-news-florida/2024-09-05/is-bankruptcy-ordeal-near-an-end-for-steward-healths-south-florida-hospitals
- Carney (Dorchester) and Nashoba Valley (Ayer, described as largely rural) closed 31 Aug 2024 (search). https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/steward-health-care-plans-close-2-massachusetts-hospitals-end-august
- Senate HELP hearing 12 Sep 2024; the CEO did not appear (Senate transcript). https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-118shrg57254/html/CHRG-118shrg57254.htm
- No source found on what Steward's directors knew, so the fix line says nothing about its board.

## Summit Peaks

**Round 1: Boeing 737 MAX. VERIFIED (committee date corrected).**
- Used on screen: Boeing had no board safety committee until 2019, and its directors settled oversight claims for $237.5M.
- Aerospace Safety Committee announced 25 Sep 2019, after the Oct 2018 and Mar 2019 crashes (search). https://www.upi.com/amp/Top_News/US/2019/09/25/Boeing-creates-safety-committee-to-oversee-plane-design-manufacture/7611569425751/
- 7 Sep 2021: Delaware Chancery denied the motion to dismiss the Caremark claims; 5 Nov 2021: $237.5M settlement paid by insurers (opened). https://www.dandodiary.com/2021/11/articles/shareholders-derivative-litigation/boeing-air-crash-derivative-lawsuit-settles-for-237-5-million/
- Final judgment Mar 2022 (search). https://lieffcabraser.com/securities/boeing/
- Death counts (189 + 157 = 346) are search-only and are not on a slide.

**Round 2: Sears pension plans. PARTLY verified. The game motion skips a payment; the real case does not claim that.**

- 1 Feb 2019: PBGC asked to take over two plans, about $1.4B underfunded, 64% funded (search). https://www.pbgc.gov/news/press/pr19-01
- Nov 2017: PBGC and Sears agreed about $500M in extra funding with real-estate liens (search). https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USPBGC/bulletins/1c33573

## Lumen Search

**Round 1: Facebook video metrics. PARTLY verified; no board angle claimed.**
- Used on screen: Facebook said in 2016 it overstated average video watch time, and paid $40M in 2019 to settle advertiser claims.
- 23 Sep 2016 apology; metric excluded views under 3 seconds; Facebook said billing was unaffected (search).
- 60 to 80 percent overstatement reported via a Publicis letter (secondary). https://thedrum.com/news/facebook-admits-it-overstated-average-video-view-times-between-60-80-two-years
- $40M settlement reported Oct 2019; Facebook called the suit without merit. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-to-pay-40-million-in-settlement-over-inflated-video-metrics/564535/ (opened)
- The "up to 900 percent" figure and the claim that engineers knew in Jan 2015 are allegations, so neither is on a slide.

**Round 2: OpenAI board removes Sam Altman. VERIFIED (letter figure corrected).**
- Used on screen: OpenAI's board removed Sam Altman, saying he was not consistently candid. 745 of 770 staff threatened to leave and he returned.
- 17 Nov 2023 statement: "not consistently candid in his communications". https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/chatgpt-maker-open-ai-pushes-out-co-founder-and-ceo-sam-altman-says-he-wasnt-consistently-candid
- 745 of 770 signed the 20 Nov letter (secondary, Wikipedia); reinstated 21 to 22 Nov. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removal_of_Sam_Altman

## Thread & Co.

**Round 1: H&M Conscious collection. PARTLY verified.**
- Used on screen: Norway's consumer authority said in 2019 that H&M's Conscious marketing lacked the information to back its claims.
- Norwegian Consumer Authority action, reported June to Aug 2019: information about the collection's sustainability was insufficient under marketing law. https://www.dezeen.com/2019/08/02/hm-norway-greenwashing-conscious-fashion-collection-news/ and https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/h-m-called-out-for-dubious-sustainability-marketing/2019061943783
- H&M said the regulator was testing whether the information was precise, not whether products were sustainable.
- June 2022: the authority warned that using the Higg MSI score would likely be misleading. https://dress-ecode.com/en/2022/06/22/hm-and-norrona-the-norwegian-authoritys-warning/
- The think piece's 2019 date holds. The Changing Markets "96 percent" figure and US lawsuit outcomes are secondary and not used.

**Round 2: Rana Plaza. Mostly VERIFIED (secondary).**
- Used on screen: Rana Plaza collapsed in Bangladesh on 24 April 2013, killing 1,134 garment workers a day after cracks appeared.
- 1,134 dead; cracks and evacuation on 23 Apr; workers told to return. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_Plaza_collapse
- Buyer-side story: social audits did not cover the building structure. The count of Accord signers depends on the date, so no number is on a slide.

## Heartland Beef

**Round 1: Wells Fargo sales practices. VERIFIED with corrections.**
- Used on screen: Wells Fargo paid $185M in 2016 over unauthorized accounts. Its board heard of the risk in 2014 but got incomplete numbers.
- 8 Sep 2016: $185M settlements; about 5,300 employees terminated Jan 2011 to Mar 2016 (SEC filing, opened). https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000072971/000119312517118654/d375947ddefa14a.htm
- Independent directors' report (10 Apr 2017): management first flagged sales practices to the board in Feb 2014; reporting was later "incomplete and inaccurate". http://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2017/05/16/with-the-benefit-of-hindsight-the-wells-fargo-sales-practices-investigation-report/
- Corrections to the think piece: about 2.1M accounts at first; 3.5M "potentially unauthorized" was a 2017 hypothetical. Not on a slide.

**Round 2: Theranos and its board. PARTLY verified.**
- Used on screen: Theranos raised over $700M. Its founder was sentenced in 2022 to 11 years 3 months. Critics noted a board of famous names with little medical expertise.
- SEC, 14 Mar 2018: charges, over $700M raised. https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2018-41
- Sentenced 18 Nov 2022 to 11 years 3 months. https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elizabeth-holmes-sentenced-theranos-fraud-11-years
- The board critique rests on press commentary (secondary). The board included a former CDC head and an industry executive, so the screen says "little", not "none". The 99.7 percent voting figure is UNVERIFIED and not used.
